**Cellular Cytoskeletal Dynamics Lab, NISER Bhubaneswar · 2021–2023**<br>
Supervised by Nivedita Mitra and Dr. Ramanujam Srinivasan

ParM is a bacterial actin homolog that segregates plasmids, including large plasmids carrying antibiotic-resistance genes, so that each daughter cell inherits a copy. ParM needs ATP to polymerise into filaments, and its partner ParR links these filaments to the plasmid.

In this project, I expressed a range of ParM homologs in a heterologous system and visualised their filaments by fluorescence microscopy, to analyse the properties of newly discovered ParMs and check their colocalisation and interaction with different ParRs. The work used DeltaVision and Leica microscopy and FRAP to probe filament dynamics, with the longer-term aim of understanding how filament dynamics differ between homologs. It formed part of my MS thesis, _Dynamic filament formation by bacterial actin homologs_ (NISER, 2023).
