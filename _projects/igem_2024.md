I served as scientific advisor to the [University of Sheffield's 2024 iGEM team](https://2024.igem.wiki/sheffield/), which was awarded a **Gold Medal**. Their project addressed the global phosphate crisis: inorganic phosphate is a finite resource, mining it generates toxic waste, and only 10–25% of applied phosphate fertiliser is taken up by crops, with the rest running off and driving eutrophication.

Rather than extracting more phosphate, the team developed a bio-reclamation strategy centred on PafA, a phosphatase from the Flavobacterium family that converts organic phosphate esters into inorganic phosphate, combined with phosphate-binding proteins (PBPs) that capture the released phosphate. They used AI-assisted protein redesign (ProteinMPNN and AlphaFold3) to improve PafA expression, thermostability and pH stability in _E. coli_, and designed an immobilised column system for simultaneous organophosphate hydrolysis and phosphate capture, with bound phosphate released by a pH change — a practical step toward scalable, sustainable phosphate recovery.

In my advisory capacity, I supported the team in navigating the intersection of molecular engineering, protein biochemistry and experimental design, helping them develop a scientifically rigorous project from concept through to implementation.
