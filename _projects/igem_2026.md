I serve as scientific advisor to the [University of Sheffield's 2026 iGEM team](https://2026.igem.wiki/sheffield/), whose project, **Dravex**, focuses on engineered exosomes for blood-brain barrier crossing and targeted gene therapy in Dravet syndrome.

Dravet syndrome is a severe, treatment-resistant childhood epilepsy, usually caused by loss of function in _SCN1A_, the gene encoding the Nav1.1 sodium channel that is especially important in GABAergic interneurons. Current treatments only manage symptoms, and gene therapies struggle to cross the blood-brain barrier, so they typically have to be injected directly into the brain or spinal cord. The team's design uses exosomes produced in HEK293 cells, displaying an RVG–Lamp2b fusion to help cross the blood-brain barrier and NRG1 to promote uptake by the affected neurons, carrying a gene therapy intended to restore functional _SCN1A_ expression. In silico modelling supports the optimisation of transport efficiency, with the aim of a minimally invasive intravenous treatment.

My advisory role spans molecular design, experimental strategy, and scientific communication — including input on construct architecture, delivery mechanism rationale, and framing of the project's translational goals. I work closely with the team to ensure their approach is both scientifically rigorous and grounded in current literature, while supporting their development as independent researchers navigating a complex, interdisciplinary problem space.

The team has raised approximately £18,000 in funding and operates across a genuinely collaborative network, working with multiple faculty members and researchers spanning several universities.
